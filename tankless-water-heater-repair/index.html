A tankless water heater does not store hot water at all. It waits for a faucet to open, senses the flow, fires a burner or energizes its elements, and heats water as it races through a compact heat exchanger. That on-demand design is a big part of its appeal, but it packs a lot of sensors and controls into one small cabinet, and every one of them has to agree before you get hot water. When one of them objects, the unit shuts down and posts an error code. In Marietta, GA, the usual culprits are mineral scale coating the heat exchanger, a flow sensor that no longer registers demand, an igniter or flame rod that cannot prove the flame, and venting or gas supply problems that trip the unit's safety limits.

Marietta Water Heater starts every tankless repair by reading the unit, not guessing at it. On every service call in Marietta, we pull the active error code and the stored fault history, then test the components that code points to: flow sensor output while a faucet runs, flame rod signal in microamps, igniter resistance, inlet gas pressure both static and at full fire, and vent condition from the unit to the termination. Scale gets checked by comparing inlet and outlet temperatures against flow rate, since a heat exchanger that cannot transfer heat gives itself away in those numbers. A code tells us where to look, not what to replace. An ignition code, for example, can come from a worn igniter, a dirty flame rod, low gas pressure, or a blocked intake, and each one calls for a different fix.

Once the cause is confirmed, most repairs are finished in a single visit. Warning Signs to Watch For

A heater that is starting to struggle rarely hides it for long, and the clues are easy to spot once you know them. Watch for these telltale signs that professional help is needed:

  • Leaks under the unit or around its water connections
  • Temperature dropping sharply when a second shower or the dishwasher starts
  • Cold slugs in the middle of hot water that last longer than before
  • New whistling, roaring, or rumbling from the unit
  • The unit clicking or trying to light several times before giving up
  • No hot water at a low-flow faucet even though the shower works fine
  • The display showing an error code instead of a temperature
  • Outlet temperature that surges and sags while water is running

Why Professional Tankless Water Heater Repair Matters

Consider the risks first. A gas-fired tankless heater can burn nearly 200,000 BTU per hour at full fire, and its vent is the only thing carrying carbon monoxide out of the house; a joint that pulls loose or an intake that gets blocked changes that in a hurry. Electric tankless models are serious electrical loads, frequently more than 100 amps spread over several 240-volt circuits. Then there is the diagnostic puzzle. Error codes point to a system, not a part, so a flow sensor fault, a scaled heat exchanger, and a bad thermistor can look nearly identical on the display, and guessing gets expensive.

Calling a pro often costs less than trial and error. Our technicians do not guess at error codes. They test flow sensors under real flow, measure flame rod signal, confirm igniter resistance, read inlet gas pressure at full fire, and check the vent from end to end. When scale is the problem, we flush it out properly and rinse the system before the unit goes back in service. And because some tankless problems, like an undersized gas line or a cracked heat exchanger, are not solved by parts at all, we tell you so directly, before you commit to anything.

Prevention Tips That Actually Help

A little routine care goes a long way toward keeping a water heater running well past its expected lifespan. Follow these tips from our technicians:

  • Never cut power to an outdoor or garage unit in winter, because it needs electricity to protect itself from freezing
  • Clear debris, snow, and dryer lint away from the vent and intake openings
  • If scale comes back quickly, add a softener or a scale-prevention cartridge on the cold inlet
  • Schedule a descaling flush every year; hard water may call for one every six months
  • Rinse the small filter screen on the cold inlet every few months
  • Get the supply line checked before adding gas appliances, which can starve a tankless unit
  • Treat a code that returns after a reset as a symptom worth diagnosing, not ignoring
