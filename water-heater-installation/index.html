Many of the installation calls we get in Marietta, GA are not replacements at all. A family adds a bathroom and needs more capacity. Someone wants the tank moved from a hall closet to the garage or basement. A remodel calls for a second unit at the far end of the house, or an all-electric conversion means trading gas for a heat pump model. Every one of them involves new work: gas lines that must be sized for the load, vents that must be routed and pitched correctly, circuits that must match the heater, and water lines that must be run cleanly. Marietta Water Heater works all of that out before the installation starts.

Good installations start on paper. We size tanks by first-hour rating and tankless units by gallons per minute at the temperature rise your incoming water needs, so the heater keeps up with your busiest hour. Location is the next question. A gas unit needs a vent path to the outside and enough combustion air. Heat pump models need room, a space that stays fairly warm, and somewhere to drain condensate. Electric tankless units can need a heavy dedicated circuit and the panel capacity behind it. Above finished space or in an attic, the heater needs a drain pan piped somewhere safe. We work through all of it with you in plain language, then give you a firm price.

On installation day, every step gets verified. We run and pressure-test new gas piping, add a sediment trap, and route venting exactly to spec. Electric and heat pump units get a circuit matched to their load. Water lines get accessible shutoffs and proper metal transitions, and the relief valve discharge runs full size to a safe point. Code items like expansion tanks, drain pans, and seismic straps are added where required, and we handle the permit when your jurisdiction needs one. Then we fill the heater, confirm draft and combustion on gas models, and set a safe temperature. Ready to start? Call (770) 215-1748 for an assessment in Marietta.

Warning Signs to Watch For

Most tanks announce trouble weeks or months ahead with rumbling noises, rusty water, or hot showers that keep getting shorter. Keep an eye out for the following:

  • Venting, relief valve piping, or gas connections that do not look right
  • Long waits for hot water at faucets far from the heater
  • A tank in a tight spot that is hard to service or replace
  • Big-tub or multi-shower demand the current heater cannot meet
  • A tank that cannot keep up with your household anymore
  • An addition too far from the existing heater for quick hot water
  • A planned switch from gas to electric, or to a heat pump model
  • A remodel that moves the utility space to a new spot

A Slow Leak Never Stays Slow

Why Professional Water Heater Installation Matters

Start with the gas line. An undersized line starves a new heater whenever the furnace or range runs, and a whole-house tankless unit usually needs more gas than the existing line carries. Vent material, size, pitch, and clearances all have to be right, or exhaust drifts back inside. Electric models need their own math on breaker, wire gauge, and panel capacity. Add relief valve piping, dielectric fittings, expansion tanks, drain pans, seismic straps, and permit rules that vary from one jurisdiction to the next, and there are a lot of details to get right.

Professional service handles every trade involved, not just setting the heater. We size by first-hour rating or flow rate, run and test the gas piping, route the vent to spec, match the circuit to the load, and add expansion tanks, drain pans, or straps where required. Before we go, gas units are checked for draft and carbon monoxide. You end up with an installation that is safe, properly sized, and ready for inspection. If a smaller or simpler heater fits your needs better, we will tell you directly.

Water Heater Care Tips From Our Technicians

A few simple habits, like an annual flush and a quick look at the relief valve, can add years to a tank. Follow these tips from our technicians:

  • Glance at the pan and discharge pipe every month or two; water there means it is time to call
  • Set the thermostat near 120 degrees for safety and efficiency
  • A periodic checkup with an anode inspection keeps a new tank healthy for years
  • Start a flushing routine early, before sediment has a chance to harden
  • Keep flammable liquids and clutter away from gas heaters
  • Keep a clear path to the heater so it can be serviced and inspected easily
  • Tankless heaters need a yearly flush to clear scale from the heat exchanger
