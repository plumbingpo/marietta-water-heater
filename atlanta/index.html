Getting More Years From Your Water Heater in Atlanta

The best way to avoid a surprise leak is routine maintenance. We recommend that homeowners flush the tank once a year (more often with hard water), have the anode rod checked every three to five years, test the relief valve once a year to make sure it isn't stuck, and keep the thermostat around 120 degrees, which limits scalding risk and cuts down on scale. Tankless owners should plan on a descaling flush once a year in hard-water areas.

Age matters too. Most tank water heaters last 8 to 12 years, tankless units often reach 20 with regular descaling, and heat pump models typically last 10 to 15. If your heater is past that window and showing warning signs such as rusty hot water, popping and rumbling, dampness under the tank, or showers that go cold sooner, a planned replacement is almost always cheaper and far less stressful than an emergency one with water on the floor.

If warning signs do show up, acting quickly keeps them small. A dripping relief valve might point to high water pressure or a missing expansion tank, both modest fixes. The same drip ignored for months can keep stressing the tank until the tank itself fails, and a small repair becomes a full replacement and a cleanup. It's smart to keep our number saved on your phone so you can reach us at the first sign of trouble. Early intervention saves time, money, and ruined floors.

Hot Water Headaches Atlanta Homeowners Face

Around Atlanta, water heater trouble usually trace back to a handful of sources. Depending on whether a home is on municipal water or a private well, minerals can be a real factor: dissolved minerals settle to the bottom of a tank as sediment, forcing the burner or lower element to work harder, and that's what causes the rumbling and popping many homeowners hear when the heater fires. Tankless units aren't immune either: scale builds inside the heat exchanger, which is why annual descaling is worth keeping up with where the water is hard.

Aging equipment is the other half of the story. A standard tank water heater usually runs 8 to 12 years, and by then the anode rod has often been eaten away, leaving the steel open to rust. Homes here also run on a mix of fuel types, natural gas and propane units in some homes and electric in others, so our technicians handle everything from failed thermocouples and draft problems to burned-out elements and failed thermostats.

Every house is set up a little differently. A home on a private well can see more sediment and occasionally a sulfur smell in the hot water, which a different anode rod often cures. A tankless unit on hard water should get annual descaling. And an older installation getting a new heater may need code items that weren't required when it went in: an expansion tank, a correctly piped relief valve line, a drain pan, or strapping where required. All of these can be fixed, and most cost far less when caught early. If something seems off, a diagnostic visit from our team can keep a small problem from turning into a flooded floor.
