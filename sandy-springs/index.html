Keeping Your Sandy Springs Water Heater Running Longer

Routine maintenance makes all the difference between a water heater that lasts and one that fails early. Our advice for homeowners is a short list: a yearly flush, an anode rod check every three to five years, a quick relief valve test once a year, a thermostat set near 120 degrees, and yearly descaling for any tankless unit on hard water, so small issues get caught early.

The age of your heater matters just as much. With regular service, a standard tank usually runs 8 to 12 years, a heat pump water heater 10 to 15, and a tankless unit up to 20. Once a heater passes that range and starts acting up, like discolored hot water, rumbling, or moisture around the base, it's smart to start planning a replacement. A planned replacement is cheaper and is far less stressful than an emergency swap after a leak.

Left alone, a small problem tends to grow. Consider a spent anode rod: replacing it is a short, inexpensive repair, but if nobody replaces it, the water starts eating the tank steel, and just like that a minor fix becomes a full replacement. That's why we encourage homeowners to keep our number in their phone and reach out at the first sign of trouble. Catching it early saves money and prevents a surprise cleanup.

Common Water Heater Problems in Sandy Springs

Around Sandy Springs, water heater trouble usually come from a handful of causes. Depending on whether a home is on municipal water or a private well, hard water may be a real factor: calcium and magnesium settle to the bottom of a tank as sediment, insulating the water from the burner, which produces the popping and rumbling many homeowners hear when the heater fires. In tankless units, the same minerals build scale inside the heat exchanger, which is why annual descaling matters so much where the water is hard.

We also see a lot of aging equipment. A standard tank water heater typically lasts 8 to 12 years, and by then the anode rod is often eaten away, leaving the steel open to rust. The area has a mix of fuel types, gas tanks in some houses and electric tanks in others, which means we service both sides: thermocouples, burners, and venting on gas units, and heating elements and thermostats on electric ones.

Every house is set up a little differently. A home on a private well can see more sediment and occasionally a sulfur smell in the hot water, which a different anode rod often cures. A tankless heater in hard water should get regular descaling. And an older installation being replaced may need updates that weren't required when it went in: an expansion tank, a properly routed relief valve line, a drain pan, or seismic straps where required. All of these can be fixed, and most cost far less when caught early. If your water heater is doing anything unusual, a quick diagnostic visit catches the small problem before it becomes an expensive one. Give us a call at (770) 215-1748 to set up a visit.
