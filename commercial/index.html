Restaurant Services

Food-Service Hot Water Systems Built for the Rush

Dish machines, sanitizing sinks, and handwashing stations all compete for the same hot water, and an undersized or failing heater shows up first as lukewarm water at the dish machine and stacks of dirty plates. A high-temperature dish machine typically needs final rinse water near 180°F, which almost always calls for a booster heater paired with the main system. Our food-service work covers high-input tanks, tankless racks, booster heaters, and peak-demand sizing, and we schedule every visit around your prep and service times.

Temperature is where many restaurant hot water problems start. If the main heater falls behind, handwashing sinks go lukewarm, the three-compartment sink falls below temperature, and the dish machine can't hit its final rinse during the rush. We verify temperatures at the tank, the booster, and the fixtures and document every visit in written reports that inspectors and landlords can review.

Replacements and maintenance visits are planned alongside owners, managers, and kitchen leads, so everything fits your operating schedule. When a job can't happen during service, we do it after close, so hot water is ready when the first shift arrives.

Safety

Code-Compliant Installation and Documentation

Badly installed or neglected hot water equipment can lead to carbon monoxide hazards, scalding, bacterial growth in tanks stored too cool, and expensive water damage from a ruptured tank. Those are real risks for a commercial property owner responsible for employees, customers, and tenants. Our commercial installations and inspections cover venting, combustion air, gas line sizing, relief valve discharge, expansion tanks, mixing valves, and code items like drain pans and seismic straps where required, followed by a written report that ranks each risk by priority and recommends specific fixes.

This work serves a dual purpose: it protects people and property, and it provides the documentation that demonstrates due diligence to landlords, insurers, and health or building inspectors. We maintain detailed service logs, temperature records, relief valve test results, and a documentation binder for every account, with more frequent service for heavy-use equipment in kitchens, laundries, and large mechanical rooms.
