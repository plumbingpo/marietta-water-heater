Getting More Years From Your Water Heater in Roswell

Most premature tank failures trace back to maintenance that never happened, and the habits that prevent them are simple. Flush the tank every year to clear sediment, get the anode rod checked every few years, and test the relief valve each year so it doesn't seize. Keep the temperature near 120 degrees for safer water and less scale. For a tankless heater in a hard-water area, plan on descaling once a year so the heat exchanger stays clean.

Age matters too. Most tank water heaters run 8 to 12 years, tankless units often reach 20 with regular descaling, and heat pump models typically last 10 to 15. If your heater is past that window and showing symptoms like discolored hot water, rumbling during heating, moisture around the base, or showers that go cold sooner, a planned replacement is almost always cheaper and much easier on you than an emergency one after the tank lets go.

Small problems rarely stay small when they're ignored. Consider a spent anode rod: replacing it is a quick, inexpensive repair, but if nobody replaces it, the water begins corroding the tank steel, and suddenly a minor fix has become a full replacement. That's why we ask homeowners to keep our number in their phone and reach out at the first hint of trouble. Catching it early saves time and money and spares you a flooded utility room.

Typical Water Heater Issues in Roswell

A water heater in Roswell rarely fails out of nowhere. Watch for rumbling and popping during heating, brownish hot water, a faint rotten-egg smell, a damp spot under the tank, or showers that run cold sooner every month. These are early signs of heavy sediment, a depleted anode, a weak element or burner, and early corrosion, and when they're caught early, they're usually an affordable repair or a planned replacement. Left alone, they end in a failed tank and water on the floor. A dripping T&P valve deserves special attention: it means the tank is seeing too much pressure or temperature, and it needs a professional look soon. We find the root cause instead of treating symptoms, so small problems stay small.
